# AIAP Open Standard Policy

**Project:** AI Avatar Protocol (AIAP)  
**Founding Author / Project Maintainer:** Wang Lu  
**Version:** 0.3.0

## 1. Open implementation

AIAP is intended to be an open interoperability standard. Any individual or organization may implement the published AIAP specification without obtaining a commercial implementation license from the AIAP Project, subject to the applicable copyright, patent, trademark, and third-party rights described in this repository.

Implementations may be open source, proprietary, commercial, non-commercial, hosted, embedded, or distributed.

## 2. Equal access

The published specification and machine-readable Schemas are intended to be available on equal terms to implementers. The AIAP Project does not require implementers to use a particular model provider, cloud provider, runtime, marketplace, or hardware platform.

## 3. Interoperability

An implementation claiming compatibility with a specific AIAP version SHOULD implement the mandatory requirements of that version and SHOULD pass the corresponding public conformance tests when such tests are available.

## 4. Extensions

Vendors may create proprietary or open Extensions. Extensions MUST NOT redefine Core semantics or claim that non-standard behavior is part of AIAP Core.

## 5. Forks and modified specifications

The specification documentation may be adapted under CC BY 4.0. Modified versions MUST be clearly identified as modified and MUST NOT imply that they are official AIAP specifications.

## 6. Certification

The right to implement AIAP is separate from the right to use any future **AIAP Certified** mark. Certification, if introduced, will be governed by a separate public certification policy and objective conformance requirements.

## 7. Trademark neutrality

Descriptive statements such as “implements AIAP v0.3” or “compatible with the AI Avatar Protocol” are permitted subject to the trademark policy, provided they are accurate and do not imply endorsement.

## 8. No platform lock-in

AIAP Core is designed so that Avatar identity and ownership are independent of a specific foundation model, Agent runtime, tool protocol, or compute provider.

## 9. Security and privacy

Open implementation does not waive security requirements. Implementers remain responsible for correct key management, authorization, privacy, data protection, secure transfer, and applicable law.
